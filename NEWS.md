# Sources — Monday, 23 November 2026

Every item on the page was reported on Monday 23 November 2026 and was
cross-checked against at least two independent outlets before being
drawn. Panel order on the page follows the list below.

## Panels

1. **Ukraine — overnight barrage on rail and power.**
   Russia attacked with 153 drones in the night of 22–23 November and
   struck railway stations in the Dnipro and Zhytomyr regions, the
   Kharkiv locomotive depot, power substations and homes; Ukraine's
   prime minister put the toll since the previous evening at 6 killed
   and 32 injured across 12 regions and Kyiv. Zelenskyy said on Monday
   that intelligence shows Russia preparing further strikes and urged
   the US to release air defences Ukraine has paid for; Kremlin
   spokesman Peskov said new trilateral talks could be expected soon.
   Ukraine also announced strikes on Russian oil refineries at Perm
   and Novoshakhtinsk on 23 November.
   — Ukrinform; Ukrainska Pravda; Interfax-Ukraine; Reuters (Japanese
   edition); UNN.

2. **Khartoum, Sudan — drones hit homes and a UN building.**
   The UN country team and the US State Department condemned drone
   strikes on residential areas and UN premises in Khartoum on
   Monday; the strikes (early Sunday) are attributed to the RSF and
   are described as the first direct hit on a UN compound since the
   army retook the capital. Sudan Tribune put the toll at at least 4
   killed, including a 10-year-old girl. The Sudanese army alleges the
   drones were launched from Ethiopia; that claim is unverified and is
   flagged as such on the page.
   — Addis Standard (reporting Sudan Tribune); UN News; Anadolu Agency.

3. **Seoul, South Korea — Lee's security reshuffle.**
   In a Monday reshuffle of security and diplomatic posts, President
   Lee Jae Myung nominated former justice minister Kang Kum-sil as
   director of the National Intelligence Service — the first woman
   nominated to lead the agency since its founding in 1961 — Lim
   Woong-soon as foreign minister and Hong Ihk-pyo as unification
   minister.
   — Reuters; Yonhap News Agency; The Korea Herald; The Korea Times;
   The Japan Times.

4. **Yerevan, Armenia — draft constitution drops Karabakh claim.**
   Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan published a draft constitution on
   Monday that removes the reference to reunification with Karabakh —
   long Azerbaijan's central condition for a formal peace treaty — and
   said a treaty would be signed once the text is adopted. He vowed to
   resign if voters reject the draft in a referendum.
   — Associated Press; Reuters (via Arab News, GMA News, Free Malaysia
   Today).

5. **Virginia & Oregon, USA — first state suits over Grok.**
   Virginia and Oregon filed the first state lawsuits against Elon
   Musk's SpaceX (formerly X Corp.) on Monday, alleging Grok's "spicy
   mode" generated sexualised and non-consensual images, including
   child sexual abuse material, with too few safeguards, in breach of
   state consumer-protection laws; both seek damages and penalties.
   — Reuters; Associated Press; Al Jazeera.

6. **Bridgetown, Barbados — Guterres calls for an AI framework.**
   Closing the Bridgetown Special Summit on AI Safety, UN
   Secretary-General António Guterres urged governments to begin talks
   within a year on a multilateral AI risk-management framework,
   warning that AI is driving "the biggest transfer of power in the
   history of humankind" from governments to a handful of technology
   companies.
   — UN News; Arab News; Caribbean National Weekly (CANA).
